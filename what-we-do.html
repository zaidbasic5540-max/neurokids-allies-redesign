Where We Create Change

Practical tools, lived experience and community allyship across four key domains.

Peers

Peer Groups & Friendships

Neurodivergent children often experience bullying, teasing and exclusion from peers who misunderstand their differences. Over time, these experiences can lead to social withdrawal and isolation, limiting opportunities to enjoy everyday childhood experiences such as playdates, birthday parties, recess and lunch breaks.

Resources Helping children understand, accept and include their neurodivergent peers.
Explore Resources
Family & Friends

Support at Home and in the Community

Being misunderstood by loved ones can be especially difficult for neurodivergent children and their families. Judgments about behaviour, communication and parenting can make family gatherings uncomfortable. Over time, invitations may become less frequent, or families may begin declining them to avoid criticism.

Resources Helping relatives, friends and loved ones better understand and support neurodivergent children and their families.
Explore Resources
Communities

Welcoming Spaces for Every Child

Faith groups, recreational facilities and community gatherings can unintentionally exclude neurodivergent children through rigid expectations, misconceptions and judgmental responses from staff, organizers and other participants.

Resources Helping community members recognize exclusionary attitudes, challenge misconceptions and become everyday allies.
Explore Resources
Policy & Research
Policy & Advocacy

Policy Contributions

Through community conversations, coffee chats, surveys and engagement with neurodivergent families, NeuroKids Allies aims to understand everyday attitudinal barriers, identify gaps and develop recommendations that promote meaningful inclusion for neurodivergent children.

Resources Combining research, lived experiences and community feedback to promote inclusive practices, evidence-based recommendations and policy development.
