A world where our attitudes create belonging for neurodivergent children.

Peers

Peer Groups & Friendships

Neurodivergent children often experience bullying, teasing and exclusion from peers who misunderstand their differences. Over time, these experiences can lead to social withdrawal and isolation, limiting opportunities to enjoy everyday childhood experiences such as playdates, birthday parties, recess and lunch breaks.

Resources Helping children understand, accept and include their neurodivergent peers.

Family & Friends

Support at Home and in the Community

Being misunderstood by loved ones can be especially difficult for neurodivergent children and their families. Judgments about behaviour, communication and parenting can make family gatherings uncomfortable. Over time, invitations may become less frequent, or families may begin declining them to avoid criticism.

Resources Helping relatives, friends and loved ones better understand and support neurodivergent children and their families.

Why NeuroKids Allies Exists

For many neurodivergent children and their families, the greatest challenges are not always the differences themselves, but how others respond to them.

A child's behaviour may be viewed as disobedience, poor manners or a reflection of inadequate parenting, rather than an expression of how they experience, process or respond to the world.

These assumptions can shape everyday interactions, making spaces that should offer connection and belonging feel unwelcoming. Over time, the cumulative impact can affect children's confidence, relationships and opportunities to participate in community life.

Neuro Kids Allies was created from the belief that inclusion requires more than simply opening doors. It requires a willingness to question our assumptions, rethink our responses and make room for different ways of being.

When we choose to understand rather than judge, we help create a world where more children can belong.

Omotayo Senbore

Founder, NeuroKids Allies • Ontario, Hamilton

About The Founder

Rooted in Lived Experience & Lifelong Advocacy

“From neuro assumptions to understanding, and from inclusion as a policy to the lived experience for each neurodivergent child.”

Omotayo is the founder of Neuro Kids Allies (NKA), an initiative inspired by her lived experiences as a neurodivergent adult and parent of a neurodivergent child.

Through her personal journey, she has witnessed how quickly children's differences can be misunderstood and how deeply the reactions of others can affect a family's sense of belonging.

These experiences shaped her belief that meaningful inclusion begins with a shift in how we see, understand and respond to people whose experiences differ from our own.

She founded Neuro Kids Allies to turn that belief into action, creating opportunities for learning, reflection and conversations that encourage people to become more thoughtful and supportive allies.

Through NKA, Omotayo hopes to inspire a culture of empathy, acceptance and everyday allyship, where neurodivergent children are valued for who they are and families can participate in community life with dignity and confidence.
